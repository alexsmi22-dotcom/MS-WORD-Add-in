Patent figures in black and white

Any chart can be rendered as 37 CFR 1.84 line art: no colour anywhere, series told apart by dash pattern and filled-or-hollow markers, with a FIG. N label and optional reference numerals on lead lines. This is the rendering an examiner accepts and the one a colour chart fails, and it is a checkbox rather than a redraw.

Inserted: FIG. 3, patent line art

Photocopy it, fax it, print it on a mono laser — the two series stay distinguishable, because nothing about the distinction was carried by colour.

Maths that shows the antiderivative

Integration is symbolic first: it returns F(x) and evaluates it exactly, falling back to Simpson only when no closed form is found — and it says which happened. Polynomials return every root including complex ones (Durand–Kerner), so “real roots in a range” is no longer a caveat you have to work around.

Alongside those: derivatives, limits (one-sided or both), systems of equations, inequalities, and word problems typed as sentences. The working is printed with the answer, because in coursework and in a methods section the derivation is the deliverable — a bare number cannot be checked by the person reading it.

It is also blunt about what defeats it. A transcendental equation still reports real roots within a range rather than claiming completeness, and an integrand with a pole inside the interval is refused rather than being handed to a grid that would quietly average across the singularity and return a confident, wrong, finite number.

∫ x² sin x dx from 0 to π F(x) = −x²cos x + 2x sin x + 2cos x value = 5.869604401089358 which is π² − 4, exactly method = exact (symbolic) x³ − 1 = 0 1, −0.5 − 0.866025404i, −0.5 + 0.866025404i method = complete (all roots)

The method line is part of the output, not a footnote — an exact answer and a numerical estimate should never look alike on the page.

Recent highlights

Some of what the last few releases added.

Tiles marked New arrived in the last few releases; the rest have grown since you last looked. Every tile opens a full breakdown with worked examples.

Periodic table & atomic structure New

An element reference that computes what can be computed and names what it cannot. Electron configuration, shell occupancy, block, group and period are generated from the aufbau rule — the Madelung order is produced from its own statement (increasing n+l, then increasing n) rather than typed out, so a transposition in 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d… is impossible rather than unlikely. The names, oxidation states, electronegativity, ionisation energy, electron affinity and radius are fetched from PubChem by a committed script that refuses to write anything unless all 118 symbols match the table this product had already verified, in order. Draws the table, a Bohr model, an orbital filling diagram, or a per-element summary.

Inserted: Bohr model, Fe

Measured configurations agree with the aufbau prediction for 88 of 118 elements, and the 30 that differ are exactly the classic exceptions — Cr, Cu, Ag, Au, U and the rest — so each source checks the other. Melting point, density, crystal structure and spectral lines are listed as absent, with the reason, rather than filled in.

Heat maps New

A numeric table as a grid of shaded cells — the comparison no other chart here makes: magnitude across two categorical axes at once, such as month × region, a correlation matrix, or an assay plate. The colour is the part that goes wrong, so it is the part that was computed. Sequential ramps are a single hue, light to dark, because a rainbow implies an ordering the eye cannot recover — is green more or less than yellow? Diverging ramps are two hues about a neutral grey, symmetric about the midpoint, which is what stops −1 and +100 reading as equally extreme. Both are run through a palette validator rather than eyeballed. A non-numeric cell is drawn blank and counted, never treated as zero — that would move the scale and shade a cell for a value that is not in your table.

Inserted: quarter × region heat map

In-cell numbers flip from black to white where the shading demands it, at a contrast threshold derived (white overtakes black at √(1.05·0.05) − 0.05) rather than picked by eye — the eyeballed one was wrong in the middle of the ramp.

Candlestick (OHLC) charts New

One candle per row from four numeric columns — where each period opened and closed, and how far it ranged in between. No other chart here carries four numbers per period: a line of closes throws the range away, and a bar of closes throws away the direction too. The convention is stated, not assumed. Green-up/red-down is a Western convention; across much of East Asia red means up. So direction is carried first by the body — hollow for up, filled for down, the older Japanese convention, which survives greyscale and a photocopier — with colour as reinforcement only, and a legend that says which is which in words. Red-is-up is available as an option, and the legend follows it either way.

Inserted: five-day OHLC

The black-and-white rendering is tested to contain no red or green at all and still tell the two directions apart. Naming the columns open/high/low/close removes the positional assumption entirely; where they are unnamed, the conventional order is read and then checked against the data.

Boundary value problems, PDEs & DAEs

Beyond the ODE solver: a two-point BVP with y fixed at both ends, the heat, wave and Laplace/Poisson equations, and semi-explicit index-1 DAEs. Each is blunt about the thing that actually bites. A BVP may have no solution, one, or infinitely many, so it says it found one and cannot tell you which case you are in — and it reports the observed convergence order from three grids as a self-check. Explicit heat stepping is stable only for r ≤ ½, so Δt is cut to satisfy that and the result says it was, instead of returning the 1e300 garbage an unstable run produces. An index-2-or-higher DAE — the Cartesian pendulum — is refused by name rather than solved into a slow drift off its own constraint.

Inserted: uₜ = uₓₓ, Crank–Nicolson

Peak 1.0000 → 0.3729 over t = 0 → 0.1. Stability number reported on every run.

Open a measured spectrum

Everything else in Spectra is predicted. This is the opposite: point it at a .jdx, .dx or .jcm file your instrument wrote and it plots exactly what the file contains, labelled measured on the chart so the two can never be confused. Read on your machine, nothing uploaded. A long trace is reduced for drawing by keeping the minimum and maximum of every bucket, so a one-point peak survives — the obvious shortcut, keeping every nᵗ point, drops narrow bands silently and leaves a tidy spectrum with a band missing.

Opened: .jdx · 4000 → 400 cm⁻¹

Your file, drawn as written — wavenumber decreases rightward, as IR is conventionally plotted.

Spectral sequences & stable homotopy

The E₂ page of a fibration, laid out as a grid with q increasing upward the way you would draw it by hand. Every possible differential is marked UNDETERMINED, never guessed. S¹ → E → S² has exactly one possible d₂, and it is an isomorphism for the Hopf fibration and zero for the trivial bundle — the same page, two different answers — so no H*(E) is claimed. Where the sequence collapses that is proved first, and what comes back is the associated graded with the extension problem named. Stable homotopy groups of spheres are a cited table (Hatcher, Toda), labelled a lookup on every result; past the tabulated range it says so rather than continuing the pattern.

Inserted: E₂ page, S¹ → E → S²

The one possible d₂ is marked, not computed — so no H*(E) is reported.

Alexander polynomial & K-theory

Δ(t) from a braid word via the reduced Burau representation, exact over ℤ[t, t⁻¹] with no floating point anywhere. Every result carries two free self-checks: the knot determinant |Δ(−1)|, which must come out odd for a knot, and the symmetry Δ(t) = Δ(1/t), which would catch a sign or index slip in the Burau matrix immediately. It also demonstrates its own weakness rather than merely stating it — the two trefoils share an Alexander polynomial where their Jones polynomials tell them apart, which is exactly why both are offered. Plus K-theory wherever Bott periodicity settles it outright: spheres, ℂPⁿ, a point, the torus.

Inserted: Δ(t) with its self-checks

trefoil Δ = 1 − t + t² |Δ(−1)| = 3 figure-8 Δ = 1 − 3t + t² |Δ(−1)| = 5 K⁰(S²) = ℤ + ℤ K¹(S²) = 0 reduced = ℤ

Symmetry check Δ(t) = Δ(1/t) passed on both; determinants odd, as a knot's must be.

Exact answers are labelled exact, numeric ones state their limits, and it refuses rather than guessing when a problem does not fit a template it can actually represent.

Private by design

Nothing you type leaves your computer.

Every tool runs locally. Structures, spectra, sequences, statistics and citations are computed on your machine, so unfiled specifications and client matters never touch a server.

There is one exception, and it is optional. The online name to structure lookup stays off until you use it, and it asks for consent before sending a compound name. Everything else works with the network disconnected.

Works on Word for Windows and macOS (Microsoft 365 or 2019 and later).
