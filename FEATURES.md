# JurisLab — What it can do

A Microsoft Word add-in fusing **STEM authoring** and **patent & legal drafting**. It
runs **entirely on your machine** — nothing you type is sent anywhere — and inserts
cleanly formatted chemistry, math, structures, figures, sequences, citations, and more
directly at the cursor.

Opening the pane shows a **Home** page with the tools grouped by category — click a card
(or a tab at the top) to open a tool: **Chemical · Mass Spec · Spectra · Bio/Assay ·
Peptide · Stats · Analyze · Math · Units · Plot · Table → Chart · Finance · Build ·
Code · Sequence · Botanical · Numerals · Refs · Citations · DNA · Reaction · Audit.**
Everything shows a live preview that matches exactly what gets inserted, and the
**Examples & syntax** panel updates to match the selected tool.

> Drafting aid — always verify structures, formulas, and listings before filing.

## 🧪 Chemical
- Format formulas with correct sub/superscripts: `H2O` → H₂O, `SO4^2-` → SO₄²⁻, `Na+` → Na⁺, `Ca(OH)2` → Ca(OH)₂.
- Charge, lone-pair, and common group/ion palette (OH, NH₄, SO₄, CH₃, …).
- **2D structures**, offline, from a **name** (`aspirin`), **formula** (`C6H6`), or **SMILES** (`CC(=O)O`).
- **Name lookup** — recognized compounds show their name (dictionary-based), which you can insert.
- **Formula validator** — checks every element symbol against the real 118-element periodic table (flags typos like `Xy`), balances parentheses/hydrates, and reports **molecular weight** and **net charge** (polyatomic ions handled, e.g. `NH4+`, `Ca(OH)2`).
- **Stereochemistry** — isomeric SMILES (`C[C@@H](N)C(=O)O`) drawn with wedges.
- **Hydrates and formula-vs-SMILES (v2.137.0)** — the hydrate coefficient is full size in preview AND insert (`CuSO4·5H2O` → CuSO₄·5H₂O, M = 249.677 g/mol); decimal hydrates are read as decimals (`CaSO4·0.5H2O` → CaO₄S·½H₂O, 145.142 g/mol — it was 226.209). A typed string that is both a formula and SMILES is read SMILES-first here with the other reading named (`CN` → "Read as SMILES: CH₅N. As a formula, “CN” would be CN, 26.018 g/mol — type formula:CN for that."); a bare formula with no single structure says so (`Fe2O3` → "a formula has no single structure to draw"). Properties come from the drawn structure, and are withheld by name where the property engine would read a different compound. A reaction typed here is pointed to Reaction (Formulas) or Solve instead of "Unexpected character".
- Each inserted structure carries provenance (formula / MW / canonical SMILES / OCL ID) in its alt-text.
- **Physicochemical properties & druglikeness** — for any resolved structure, a readout of **cLogP**, **logS**, **topological PSA**, **H-bond donors/acceptors**, **rotatable bonds**, and **heavy-atom count**, plus the **Lipinski Rule of Five** and **Veber** oral-druglikeness screens (with the specific criteria that pass/fail). Insert the summary at the cursor. Estimated offline (OpenChemLib) — no server, no license tier.
- **Online name resolution (opt-in)** — for a full **systematic IUPAC name** the offline dictionary doesn't know (e.g. *2-amino-3-(1H-indol-3-yl)propanoic acid*), a **“Resolve name online”** button parses it via the EMBL-EBI **OPSIN** service and draws the result (also reporting its **InChIKey**). This is the **only** feature that leaves your machine: it's off until you click it, shows a **consent prompt** naming the service before the name is sent over the internet, and warns against use for confidential names. Everything else stays fully offline.

## ⚛️ Mass Spec — exact mass, isotope pattern, adducts
- From a **name, formula, or SMILES**, an offline mass-spectrometry readout for proteomics / metabolomics / small-molecule MS.
- **Exact masses** — **monoisotopic** (for high-resolution MS) and **average** molecular weight.
- **Isotope pattern — every stable isotope (v2.139.0)** — nominal-mass peaks with relative intensities for all **84 elements with a natural isotopic composition (H–U)**: masses AME2020, compositions CIAAW 2024, stored verbatim and hashed. Peaks are labelled from **M = the most abundant isotope of every element**, so M is not always the tallest or the leftmost: `C2Cl4` → base peak **M+2 165.8725 (100.0)**, M 163.8754 (78.1), M+4 167.8695 (48.0); ferrocene → **M−2 184.0179 (6.37)** for ⁵⁴Fe, with a true minus. Chlorophyll a's magnesium is now IN its pattern (M+1 74.63, M+2 41.69). Every result says what it leaves out — "Nominal-mass peaks (no fine structure) … 2 further peaks below 0.1 % of the base not listed." — and where its numbers come from, on screen and in the inserted text. Drawn as an **isotope stick chart** you can insert. Natural composition only (no enrichment), no fine structure.
- **Labelled isotopes** — `[13CH3]C` → "Labelled isotopes: ¹³C × 1"; `C2H5D` → "D read as deuterium, ²H (2.014102 u) — a named isotope, not natural hydrogen."; `Tc` → "Tc (technetium) has no stable isotope … Name the isotope as SMILES, e.g. [99Tc] for ⁹⁹Tc." and `[99Tc]` → 98.9062.
- **Compare with an acquired mass spectrum (v2.139.0)** — open a JCAMP-DX MS file and the computed pattern is drawn head-to-tail beneath it with a match table: 2-chlorophenol against a measured EI spectrum → M 128.003 ↔ 128.000, M+2 130.000 ↔ 130.000, "4 of 5 predicted lines have a measured peak inside their window."
- **Adduct m/z** — the common ESI ions computed exactly: **[M+H]⁺, [M+Na]⁺, [M+K]⁺, [M+NH₄]⁺, [M+2H]²⁺, [M−H]⁻, [M+Cl]⁻, [M+HCOO]⁻, [M−2H]²⁻**.
- **Formulas are formulas (v2.137.0)** — `Co` is cobalt (monoisotopic 58.9332), no longer CH₃O; `Fe2O3` has masses (159.8546) though it has no structure; `CO` → "Read as the formula CO. As SMILES, “CO” would be methanol (CH₄O) — write smiles:CO for that." The formula shown carries its charge (MnO₄⁻).
- Insert the full readout as a text summary. Computed entirely on your machine — verify before relying.

## 📡 Spectra — predicted NMR, IR, UV-Vis & fragmentation
- From a **name, formula, or SMILES**, predicted spectra computed entirely offline. **These are estimates from published additivity rules — not acquired spectra, and not quantum-chemical calculations.** Structure recognition is exact (an ester is never mistaken for a ketone); the numbers are empirical and every prediction states its own accuracy.
- **¹H NMR** — chemical shift, **integration**, **n+1 multiplicity** (s/d/t/q/quint…), and an assignment per signal. Symmetry-aware, so benzene is one 6H singlet, not six signals. Exchangeable **OH/NH/COOH** protons are reported as nominal ranges and flagged *variable*, because they genuinely depend on solvent and concentration. Typical accuracy **±0.2–0.4 ppm**.
- **Diastereotopic groups (v2.139.0)** — read from the structure's topology. Ibuprofen's ArCH₂ is one row reported as **"m (²J 14–18 Hz)"** — the typical geminal range for a CH₂ next to a π system, never the vicinal 7 Hz — and its isobutyl CH₃ are two rows at δ 0.87, each with the note "diastereotopic: no symmetry operation makes this group equivalent to its partner at the same δ, so the two can differ; the difference is not predicted …". Notes are footnoted in the table and travel into the document. Topology only: no Δδ, no second-order patterns, no Karplus; a molecule too large to enumerate (squalane, four unspecified stereocentres) says "relative configurations were NOT enumerated (over the work cap)".
- **¹³C NMR** — proton-decoupled shifts by Grant–Paul additivity (sp³) and benzene substituent increments (aromatic), with carbonyl classes properly distinguished (ketone ≈ 205, aldehyde ≈ 199, acid ≈ 178, ester ≈ 171, amide ≈ 172). Typical accuracy **±2–4 ppm**.
- **IR** — characteristic **group frequencies** with published ranges and qualitative intensities, plus a simulated **transmittance trace**. Carbonyl classes separate correctly (ester ≈ 1740 > ketone ≈ 1715 > amide ≈ 1660) and conjugation lowers C=O ~25 cm⁻¹ (acetophenone vs acetone). The **fingerprint region below ~1500 cm⁻¹ is not predicted** — it is compound-specific, and no additivity scheme reproduces it.
- **UV-Vis** — **λmax** by the **Woodward–Fieser rules** for conjugated dienes and α,β-unsaturated carbonyls, showing **every increment** so the arithmetic is auditable. Within its domain, ~±5 nm. Outside it the tool says so rather than inventing a number: an unconjugated molecule is reported **transparent** (absorbs below ~200 nm), and benzenoid/isolated-carbonyl bands come from tabulated values, explicitly not Woodward–Fieser. **v2.139.0:** every enone arm is scored and the longest reported (prednisone: "2 enone arms were each scored (239, 227 nm)"); an extended enone gains +30 nm per extending C=C (`CC=CC=CC(=O)C` → 263 nm) with an extended-accuracy caveat in place of ±5 nm; aryl enones (cinnamaldehyde, chalcone) and quinones are **refused by name** ("quinone chromophore — not a Woodward-Fieser enone").
- **MS fragmentation (EI)** — principal fragments with **exact** m/z, via the classical pathways: **α-cleavage** (acylium, oxocarbenium/iminium), **benzylic → tropylium**, allylic, **McLafferty rearrangement** (only when a γ-hydrogen actually exists), and neutral losses (H₂O, CO₂, CO, NH₃, HCl) gated on the group that enables them. Correctly ranks the real base peak for toluene (m/z 91), ethanol (31), butylamine (30) and 2-pentanone (43/58). **Likelihood is a rule-based ranking, never a predicted intensity.**
- Insert either a **data table** or a **spectrum chart** (NMR/IR/MS; δ and wavenumber axes run in the conventional direction). Every insert carries the caveat with it.
- **Compare with acquired spectrum (v2.139.0)** — open a measured JCAMP-DX file and overlay the prediction on it (sticks for NMR, a curve for IR, a λmax marker for UV, head-to-tail for MS), with a match table of each predicted line, the nearest measured peak and Δ. 2-methyl-1-propanol ¹H against its measured spectrum → "4 of 4 predicted lines have a measured peak inside their window." Referencing is never shifted silently: an ethylbenzene ¹³C file is "Not assessed: the measured axis looks offset by +9.09 ppm. Give a reference peak, then compare." — give `86.25 = 77.16` and it is 6 of 6. Every table and figure says "A prediction is not an assignment: a nearest peak is proximity, not identity." No baseline correction, phasing or integration.
- **JCAMP-DX reader (v2.139.0)** — a multi-block file shows the reader's own choice with its reason and a block picker; FID ("NMR FID — needs Fourier transform and phasing; export the processed spectrum"), nD NMR and GC-MS NTUPLES files are refused by name; a block that fails its own checks (a line X that disagrees with the declared spacing → "X-check failed …", a DIF y-check) is shown with the failing line and cannot be inserted or overlaid; a peak table is drawn as sticks, never joined into a trace; units are shown as cm⁻¹, Hz, m/z with the file's raw string kept as provenance.
- Known limits, disclosed in-pane: fused aromatics (naphthalene-type) and heteroaromatic rings are approximate; carbons bearing 3+ electron-withdrawing groups over-count; ring fragmentation needs two cleavages and is covered only via the listed rearrangements.

## 🏗️ Engineering — a full undergraduate engineering bench, in Word
- 241 calculators over your own numbers, offline — now including **Reliability**: Weibull life fitted by **maximum likelihood** from your own test data, with units still running entered as suspensions and used IN the fit rather than discarded (throwing survivors away biases the fitted life short). The shape parameter is the answer rather than the mean life — below 1 the parts are dying young and burn-in helps, above 1 they are wearing out and scheduled replacement helps, at 1 neither does anything — and the regime is read from a **likelihood-ratio interval**, so an interval straddling 1 is reported as a constant hazard however far above 1 the point estimate sits. Series and parallel **block diagrams** naming the component that carries most of the failures, **k-out-of-n** for voting logic and pump trains, **active against standby spares** with the two assumptions that make standby look better stated where the numbers are, and **availability** labelled INHERENT because it counts the repair and not the wait for a spare or a technician. Every failure rate, MTBF and MTTR is your input; there is **no built-in failure-rate handbook**, for the same reason there is no Cd table. Plus **Trajectory & orbits**: projectile motion with and without air drag (45° is optimal *only* when launch and landing heights match; drag is the dominant term, not a correction), the inverse aiming problem returning **both** the flat and the lofted angle, impact energy that **saturates** at the terminal-speed ceiling, circular and elliptical orbits, the Hohmann transfer (**to catch something ahead of you, slow down**), the rocket equation, escape speed, jerk-limited S-curve profiles, multi-axis coordination, great-circle navigation and the wind triangle. Gravitational parameters are IAU 2009 values script-extracted from a published source and cross-checked; drag coefficients are **your** input. **Exact where exactness is real, floating point where it is not, and it always says which.**
- **Beam analysis** — reactions, shear force and bending moment diagrams, and deflection, from a span, its supports (pin/roller/fixed) and its loads (point, uniform, linearly varying, applied couple). **Statically indeterminate beams are the same code path** — a propped cantilever needs no force method and no superposition table — and the reactions come out as exact rationals, so a propped cantilever under a UDL reports **3/8 wL**, not 0.37499999996. Deflection needs EI; on rigid supports nothing else does, because for a prismatic beam the flexural rigidity divides out. **Supports may be elastic or displaced** — `roller 8 k=5e4` sits one on a spring, `roller 8 settle=0.01` sinks it — and both stay exact over rationals. Those two cases *do* need EI, and the tool says why rather than quietly accepting it: a spring or a settlement makes the compatibility conditions non-homogeneous, so the reactions genuinely depend on the beam's own stiffness. On a **determinate** beam neither changes any reaction at all — the beam just moves — which is the classic result and the oracle the tests pin it against. Combining both on one support means the **seat** drops by the settlement and the spring then compresses on top of it (`v = -settle - R/k`), a settling elastic foundation rather than a beam pinned to that level regardless of spring force; the two are different models and the docs say which one this is. **Every numeric field takes an exact fraction** — `roller 8/3`, `point 30 at 8/3`, `udl 7/2 from 1/3 to 16/3` — which matters here more than it would elsewhere: the engine computes over rationals, so a support at *L*/3 typed as 2.6666666667 puts a rounding error into the input of an exact solver, while `8/3` returns exactly 20.
- **Cross-section properties & stress** — area, second moment, section modulus, radius of gyration and the first moment Q, plus peak bending and transverse shear stress. A non-symmetric section reports **both** section moduli, because quoting one number for a tee understates the fibre that yields first.
- **Stress state** — principal stresses, principal angle, Mohr's circle centre and radius, von Mises and Tresca, and the factor of safety against a yield strength. Reports the **absolute** maximum shear alongside the in-plane one and flags when they differ: for a biaxial state the zero out-of-plane principal lies outside the in-plane pair, so the in-plane circle understates the real shear (σ1 = 100, σ2 = 60 → 20 in-plane, **50 in truth**). Full 3D states are solved in closed form from the tensor invariants.
- **Truss analysis (method of joints), solved exactly.** Solving for force *per unit length* instead of axial force keeps every matrix entry rational, so the equilibrium solve is exact and the only square root happens once per member at reporting time — **reactions are exact always**, and zero-force members are exact zeros rather than values under a tolerance. A **mechanism**, a **statically indeterminate** truss, and a **critical form** (member count balances, structure still collapses — what counting members cannot catch) are each named instead of answered.
- **Column buckling** — Euler **with the Johnson parabola** for short columns, so a stocky column is never quoted a critical load that would need a stress above yield. Reports effective length, slenderness, the transition slenderness, the squash load, and which curve governs.
- **Shaft torsion** — polar second moment, peak and bore shear stress, angle of twist. **Circular sections only, on purpose:** τ = Tr/J is a theorem for a circle and false for a rectangle, which warps.
- **Circuits** — modified nodal analysis from a SPICE-style netlist. The **DC operating point is exact** over rationals (a divider reports 10/3 V), values may be written as a schematic prints them (`2k2`, `4R7`), and AC gives phasors at a frequency or sweeps a node into a **Bode magnitude plot**. Refusals are circuit errors, not matrix errors: a node with no DC path to ground is named, as are two ideal sources fighting.
- **Pipe flow & head loss** — Reynolds number, **Colebrook-White solved rather than approximated** (bounded, convergence-checked iteration), Darcy-Weisbach head loss, minor losses, wall shear, pressure drop and pump power, with built-in roughness and water-property tables. Laminar flow uses f = 64/Re and says the roughness was ignored. The **transition band (2300 < Re < 4000) returns a figure and states that it is unreliable**, because no correlation there is trustworthy.
- **Composite wall / pipe insulation** — a thermal resistance chain with every interface temperature, each layer's share, and the controlling layer. For a cylinder it reports the **critical radius**: below k/h, adding insulation makes the heat loss *worse*, and it says so.
- **Heat exchanger (LMTD sizing)** — counter or parallel flow, area from duty or duty from area. Equal terminal differences are handled as the removable singularity they are (the naive formula gives NaN for a perfectly ordinary balanced exchanger), and a temperature cross is reported as normal in counterflow and refused as impossible in parallel flow.
- **Control systems** — **transfer functions** entered as coefficients highest power first (`1 3 2`) or written out (`s^2+3*s+2`).
  - **Poles, zeros & stability** with the **exact** Routh-Hurwitz array. Routh is a tabulation of differences of products, so a coefficient that is 1e-17 instead of 0 flips the verdict — and floating point is least reliable exactly at the stability boundary, the only place anyone runs it. Over rationals it is exact.
  - **Stability is decided twice and cross-checked.** The right-half-plane pole count comes once from the exact tabulation and once from the poles as eigenvalues of the companion matrix; they share no arithmetic, so agreement is evidence rather than the same mistake twice. **When they disagree, both are reported and neither is chosen** — that means a pole is close enough to the axis that its computed real part cannot be trusted, which is the case a single method answers confidently and wrongly.
  - **Step & impulse response** — damping ratio, natural frequency, overshoot, rise, peak and settling time, with a plot. Exact identities for a genuine second-order system; above that they come from the dominant pole pair and the result **says so**, more loudly when the next pole is under five times faster. A zero near the dominant poles is flagged, because the standard formulas know nothing about zeros.
  - **Frequency response & margins** — Bode magnitude and phase, gain and phase margin. **A margin that does not exist is reported as not existing:** a first-order lag never reaches −180°, so its gain margin is infinite and any finite number — including the value at the edge of the swept range — is wrong.
  - **PID & closed loop** — controller in series, loop closed, with closed-loop poles, stability, margins and transient. A **right-half-plane zero** is named as non-minimum-phase: the step response goes the *wrong way* first and more gain makes it worse.
  - Refused rather than faked: an **improper** transfer function has no state-space realisation and is not simulated as something else; a factored input (`(s+1)(s+2)`) is refused rather than mis-parsed into a different plant.
- **Pharmacokinetics** — built on **clearance and volume**, the physiologically independent parameters; half-life is a consequence of both (t½ = ln2·Vd/CL), which is why a renal-failure patient and an obese patient get a long half-life for completely different reasons.
  - **Dose & concentration curve** — IV bolus, infusion or oral, with Cmax, Tmax, AUC and a plot. **AUC = Dose/CL**, so total exposure is set by clearance alone and *not* by volume; the infusion plateau is rate/CL, and volume only sets how fast you reach it.
  - **Steady state & loading dose** — accumulation ratio, peak, trough, average and fluctuation. The average depends **only** on dose rate and clearance, so halving both dose and interval leaves it unchanged and only narrows the swing. Time to steady state depends **only** on half-life and a bigger maintenance dose does not shorten it — which is exactly what a loading dose is for, and why the loading dose comes from the volume while the maintenance dose comes from the clearance.
  - **Non-compartmental analysis** of measured data — paste `time concentration` pairs for λz, half-life, AUC, CL, Vz and MRT. The terminal window is **chosen** by trying every window of ≥3 points and keeping the best *adjusted* R², and the **percentage of AUC that came from extrapolation** is reported: above ~20% the study did not follow the drug long enough and every derived parameter rests on an assumed tail.
  - **Flip-flop kinetics are detected.** When absorption is slower than elimination the terminal slope is *absorption*, so a half-life read off the tail is the absorption half-life and every parameter from it is wrong — and the curve looks entirely normal either way. Common in depot injections and modified-release formulations.
  - Oral data reports **CL/F and Vz/F**, never "clearance": without an IV reference, bioavailability cannot be separated, and a drug with 50% F would look as though it clears twice as fast as it does. Every model here is **linear** — saturable elimination (phenytoin) breaks all of it, and that is stated rather than assumed.
- **Vibration** — single- and multi-degree-of-freedom, built around the three results that are most often got wrong. **Multi-DOF forced response** by modal superposition reports amplitude and phase at every degree of freedom plus the modal breakdown, so you can see which mode is actually carrying the load: **every** natural frequency is a resonance, not just the first, and a force applied at a **node** of a mode cannot excite it at all. Damping is entered as modal ratios (or Rayleigh α, β) because the method assumes **classical damping** — which a single discrete damper between two floors does not satisfy — and it states that rather than assuming it of a matrix you supplied.
  - **Resonance is not at r = 1.** The magnification peaks at r = √(1−2ζ²), always *below* the natural frequency, and for **ζ ≥ 0.707 there is no peak at all** — no forcing frequency can resonate the system. "Resonance at ω = ωₙ" is the undamped special case, taught first and then never unlearned.
  - **Isolation only begins above r = √2.** Transmissibility is exactly 1 at √2 for *every* damping ratio and above 1 below it, so a mount that is not soft enough does not isolate a little — it **amplifies**, and more force reaches the floor than if the machine were bolted down.
  - **Damping helps below √2 and hurts above it.** More damping lowers the resonant peak (which is why you want it on run-up) and *raises* transmissibility in the isolation region, because the damper is itself a path for force.
  - **Free response & damping** — ωₙ, ζ, ωd, logarithmic decrement and a plot. Each damping regime uses its **own** closed form: the critically damped case is not the underdamped one evaluated at ζ = 1, which divides by a zero damped frequency. Two measured peaks estimate ζ from a recorded trace by the **exact** relation, not the light-damping approximation.
  - **Natural frequencies & mode shapes** — from a chain of masses and springs or from M and K directly, solved as a **symmetric** generalised eigenproblem (K φ = ω²M φ via a Cholesky transform) so the eigenvalues are guaranteed real; forming M⁻¹K and using a general solver discards that and can return complex frequencies for an ordinary structure. Shapes come back **mass-normalised** with a fixed sign convention. A **rigid-body mode** is reported as the real feature it is, and a mass matrix that is not positive definite is refused as unphysical — the numerical failure and the modelling error are the same event.
- **Thermodynamics** — ideal-gas processes, air-standard power cycles, and the Carnot limits that bound every one of them.
  - **Temperature is absolute, always, and it is enforced.** Every efficiency and entropy here is a ratio of absolute temperatures, so you pick the unit you are typing and it converts before anything is divided. The Carnot bound between 500 °C and 20 °C is 62% correctly and **96%** if the numbers go in as written — a plausible, publishable, completely wrong figure, and the commonest error in the subject. A temperature at or below absolute zero is refused rather than divided by.
  - **Ideal-gas process** — isothermal, isobaric, isochoric, isentropic and polytropic are **one family** (P·Vⁿ = constant, with n = 1, 0, ∞ and k), so the work integral is derived once and cannot disagree between them. The n = 1 singularity is written as its own limit branch rather than left to divide by zero. Every result carries a first-law check, Q = ΔU + W.
  - **Isentropic is not the same as adiabatic** — adiabatic means only that no heat crosses the boundary; isentropic means adiabatic *and reversible*, and a real adiabatic compression ends hotter for the same pressure ratio. That gap is what an isentropic efficiency measures, and the two words are conflated constantly.
  - **Power cycles** — Otto, Diesel and Brayton, air-standard, each compared against the Carnot bound between its own extremes. At the *same* compression ratio **Otto beats Diesel** — and it explains why Diesel wins in practice anyway (no fuel in the cylinder during compression, so nothing to knock, so a much higher ratio is available). Brayton efficiency depends only on pressure ratio, and the **work-optimum** ratio is not the efficiency-optimum one, which is why real turbines are sized where they are.
  - **Rankine and vapour-compression refrigeration take enthalpies you look up in your own tables.** No steam tables are built in, **deliberately**: a saturated-water table reconstructed from memory would be plausible, unverifiable and wrong in the third digit somewhere nobody would check, and this product's rule is that all data must be real. The tool does the cycle arithmetic, the back-work ratio and the energy-balance check on *your* property data.
  - **Carnot check** — paste any claimed efficiency or COP with two reservoir temperatures and it says whether it is thermodynamically possible, naming the Celsius error when it is not. And a COP above 1 is not a violation of anything: a refrigerator *moves* heat rather than making it.
  - **The gas table stores only cp and cv; R and k are derived from them.** Quoting all four independently makes them mutually inconsistent — air's usual cp = 1005, cv = 718, R = 287, k = 1.4 give R/(k−1) = 717.5, not 718 — and the isentropic work integral then disagrees with the internal-energy change by 0.07%, which shows up as an **apparent first-law violation** caused entirely by rounding. Deriving them makes every identity exact.
- **Fatigue & machine design** — the natural successor to the stress tools: you have a stress state, and the next question is whether the part survives 10⁷ cycles.
  - **Every fatigue life is an order of magnitude, not a number.** Identical specimens from the same bar, tested on the same machine, routinely differ by a **factor of three** in life. Quoting a fatigue life to three significant figures is a category error, and every result says so. This is the most over-trusted calculation in mechanical engineering and the module is written accordingly.
  - **The endurance limit does not exist for most materials.** Steel has a genuine knee in the S-N curve below which it survives indefinitely. **Aluminium, copper and magnesium do not** — their curve keeps falling, so there is no stress with infinite life at any level. Choose the material class and a non-ferrous one is told this instead of being handed a false infinite.
  - **Corrected endurance limit** by the Marin factors (surface, size, load, temperature, reliability), with the largest single reduction named — it is almost always the **surface finish**, where an as-forged surface more than halves what a ground one gives. The reliability factor is **derived** by inverting the normal CDF rather than tabulated at five points, so any reliability works. A creep temperature is refused outright, because above ~450 °C the failure mode is not fatigue.
  - **Goodman alone can pass a part that yields on the first cycle.** No fatigue criterion knows about static yield, so the **Langer** check runs alongside every one and the *governing* (smaller) factor of safety is reported, with which one governs. All four criteria — Goodman, Soderberg, Gerber, ASME-elliptic — are shown together, because they disagree by a lot and the spread is the honest uncertainty in the method.
  - **Compressive mean stress is treated as zero, not negative** — it holds cracks closed and helps (exactly why shot peening works), and feeding it in negative would *overstate* the benefit rather than merely ignore it.
  - **Finite life and Miner cumulative damage** over a load spectrum. Miner ignores load ORDER, and observed damage sums at failure scatter between about 0.3 and 3 — so a sum of 0.9 is not a pass. Blocks below the endurance limit contribute nothing, which is standard practice and optimistic: once a crack has started, the small cycles are not free.
  - **Sut and Sy are asked for, never looked up** — same principle as the steam tables. They move by a factor of three with heat treatment for the same alloy designation, so a table of typical values would be wrong for the specific piece of steel in front of you.
- **Electronics** — beyond the linear circuit solver.
  - **Op-amp circuits** — inverting, non-inverting, buffer, summing, difference, integrator and differentiator, with the limits the ideal model hides. **Bandwidth is the price of gain**: a 1 MHz gain-bandwidth part at a gain of 100 leaves 10 kHz, and the *noise* gain sets it, so a unity-gain inverting stage is half as fast as it looks. **Slew rate is a separate, larger-signal limit** and the full-power bandwidth is usually far below the small-signal one. An **ideal integrator saturates** — with no DC feedback path it integrates its own offset to a rail — which is why the textbook 1/(sRC) never appears in a working schematic.
  - **Analogue filter design** — Butterworth and Chebyshev I, low- or high-pass. The **minimum order is computed from the specification** and rounded up, the **delivered** stopband attenuation is reported alongside the one asked for, and **both families are compared** so the trade is visible: Butterworth is maximally flat and therefore the slowest to roll off; Chebyshev meets the same spec at a lower order and pays in passband ripple. The output is a **transfer function**, so it goes straight to the control tools — and it says to look at the *phase* before committing, because that is the half a magnitude plot hides.
  - **Truth tables and Boolean minimisation** — write the expression in any common notation (`A AND B`, `A&B`, `AB`, `A*B`, trailing apostrophe for NOT). Minimisation is **Quine-McCluskey**, so it does not run out at five variables the way a Karnaugh map does. Essential prime implicants are identified, **don't-cares** are used and the risk of them being reachable is stated, and when several minimal forms exist it says so — a textbook answer of the same size is not a different result.
- **Fluids breadth** — beyond pipe flow.
  - **Open-channel flow** by Manning, for rectangular, trapezoidal, triangular and part-full circular sections. **The Froude number is the answer, not the discharge**: it decides whether the channel is controlled from upstream or downstream, and crossing Fr = 1 unintentionally produces a **hydraulic jump** that will eat an unprotected bed. The Froude number uses the *hydraulic depth* A/T, not the flow depth — they differ for every non-rectangular section. Manning's n is given as a **range**, because that range is the answer: discharge is inversely proportional to it and published values span a factor of two.
  - **Pump NPSH and cavitation** — cavitation is a **failure mode, not an efficiency loss**, and it is entirely a suction-side problem that nothing downstream can fix. **NPSH available falls as the liquid gets hotter**, which is why a pump that ran for years on cold water cavitates the first warm summer.
  - **Compressible flow** — isentropic relations, with **choking**: past the critical pressure ratio (0.528 for air) lowering the downstream pressure does *not* increase the mass flow, because that information cannot travel upstream against sonic flow. Supersonic area–velocity behaviour is backwards from intuition and it says so.
- **Biomedical engineering** — where this product's engineering and life-science halves meet.
  - **Haemodynamics** — Poiseuille resistance, wall shear stress and Reynolds number in a vessel, plus systemic vascular resistance from MAP, CVP and cardiac output. **The fourth power is the whole story**: a 20% narrowing more than doubles resistance and halving the radius multiplies it by sixteen, which is why arterioles control blood pressure with tiny diameter changes and why a modest-looking stenosis is not modest. **Wall shear stress is a biological signal** — endothelium remodels toward 1–2 Pa, and persistently low or oscillating shear is exactly where plaque forms.
  - **Joint biomechanics** — the body is built almost entirely from **third-class levers**, so the muscle force is many times the external load; and the **joint reaction force** is larger than either, because the muscle presses the bones together while the load pulls them apart. That is the number a prosthesis has to survive, and the one most often left out.
  - **Sampling and aliasing** — checks a sampling rate against the signal. **Aliasing is irreversible**: a component above Nyquist folds down and becomes indistinguishable from real data, so the anti-alias filter must be analogue and before the converter. Nyquist is a lower bound, not a design target. And frequency resolution depends **only on record length**, not on sampling rate — the other half of the trade nobody states.

## 🧮 Analyze — no-code numerical workbench
- Paste numbers, get real computed results and publication-ready figures straight into Word. No scripting, no licence, fully offline.
- **Linear algebra** — solve **A·x = b**, inverse, determinant, rank, trace, transpose, multiply; **eigenvalues** (symmetric via Jacobi, general via Francis double-shift QR **including complex pairs**), **QR** (Householder) and **SVD** (one-sided Jacobi). Results insert as Word tables.
- **Matrix expressions** — one line, e.g. `A*inv(B)+2*C'`.
- **Optimization** — unconstrained minimization (Nelder–Mead).
- **FFT** — radix-2 FFT with zero-padding, frequency spectrum and dominant-peak detection, inserted as a chart.
- **ODE / system solving** — type the equation you actually have; two integrators with **automatic stiffness detection** pick themselves.
  - **Higher order is reduced for you.** `y'' = -0.1*y' - y` with `y = 1, y' = 0` just works — no hand-reduction to a first-order system. Third order and above too, several higher-order equations at once, and mixed orders in one system. The result names the states it created.
  - **Explicit RK45** (adaptive Dormand–Prince). Fast and highly accurate on well-behaved problems — ~8 significant figures on y′ = −y in ~23 steps.
  - **Implicit RODAS4** (4th-order Rosenbrock, L-stable and stiffly accurate; one LU factorization per step, no Newton iteration). This is what makes **stiff** systems solvable — *stiffness is the normal case in chemical kinetics whenever rate constants differ by orders of magnitude*. Van der Pol at μ=1000 (~1,500 steps) and **Robertson kinetics** (rate constants spanning 0.04 → 3×10⁷; ~570 steps, mass conserved to 12 digits) both solve; the explicit solver cannot finish either.
  - **Auto** starts explicit and switches to implicit mid-integration if the problem stiffens (so Van der Pol, which *starts* non-stiff, is still solved). The result states which solver ran.
  - **Rich right-hand sides** — `t`, the state names, and a full function library: trig, inverse trig, hyperbolics (`tanh`), logs to any base, `sqrt`/`cbrt`, rounding, `min`/`max`/`clamp`, true `mod`, `hypot`, a Heaviside `step`, comparisons (`<`, `>=`, `==` …) and **`if(cond, a, b)`** for piecewise/switching inputs.
  - **Report at the times you choose** — a list (`0, 1, 2.5`) or a range (`0:0.5:10`). The solver is forced to *land* on each one, so those values are **computed, not interpolated** — there is no interpolation error to discount. The plot still uses every step, so the curve stays smooth.
  - **Stop on a condition** — answer "when does it…?" rather than reading it off a chart. Give an expression that crosses zero: `z` for "when it hits the ground", `y - 100` for a threshold, `y'` for "at the turning point". The crossing is located by bisection to solver tolerance and the solution **ends at the event** — e.g. a projectile `z'' = -9.81` from `z' = 20` stops at t = 4.077472 with z = 0 and z′ = −20, matching the closed form exactly.
  - Honest limits: numerical only — no symbolic/closed-form solutions, no boundary-value problems, no PDEs. Ultimate accuracy is capped near 1×10⁻¹² by the finite-difference Jacobian (no analytical Jacobian is supplied).
- **Raw data → insights** — per-column summaries, Tukey outlier & missing-data flags, **Pearson + Spearman** correlations with p-values, trend/slope detection, and a plain-language "what this means" write-up. Computed from your numbers — never invented.

- **λ-calculus** — the untyped λ-calculus, reduced **step by step**: every β-step lists the redex it contracted, and a binder that would capture a free variable is **renamed first** as an α-step of its own (`(λx.λy.x) y` → `λy₁.y`, never `λy.y`). **Normal order** by default — the strategy that reaches a normal form whenever one exists — with **applicative order** (call-by-value) by name, so the textbook separation is shown rather than told: `K I OMEGA` answers `λx.x` one way and provably diverges the other. Caps are stated by name (60 vertices, 500 edges, 40 states, 10 variables).

When the sequence **collapses** that is **proved** first, and what you get is the associated graded with the extension problem named. **Stable homotopy groups of spheres** are a **cited table** (Hatcher, Toda), labelled as a lookup on every result, and outside the tabulated range it says "not tabulated here" instead of continuing the pattern.
- **Boundary value problems, PDEs and DAEs** — y'' = f(x, y, y') with y fixed at **both** ends (finite differences or shooting); the **heat**, **wave** and **Laplace/Poisson** equations by finite differences; and semi-explicit **DAEs of index up to 5** (v2.138.0, below). Each is honest about the thing that actually bites: a BVP may have **no solution, one, or infinitely many** and this says it reports one and cannot tell which; explicit heat stepping is stable only for r ≤ 1/2, so Δt is **reduced to satisfy it and the result says so** rather than returning the 1e300 garbage an unstable run produces; and a DAE's drift off its own constraints is **measured and printed**, never asserted away. BVP solutions are computed on three grids and report the **observed convergence order** as a self-check.
- **DAE index reduction (v2.138.0, Analyze → Differential-algebraic system)** — type the system you have; **Pantelides' algorithm** finds the structural index (≤ 5, ≤ 12 unknowns), differentiates the constraints itself, and **confirms the index numerically** at t₀. ## 📐 Stats — statistics & uncertainty
- Turn experimental data into a paper-ready result, offline. Pick a test, paste numbers (separated by spaces, commas, or new lines), and it computes live.
- **Descriptive statistics** — n, mean, SD, **SEM**, variance, median, min/max, **95% CI** (t-based), CV.
- **t-tests** — two-sample (**Welch** or **Student** pooled) and **paired**, each with a real **p-value** and an APA-style report (*t*(18) = 2.41, *p* = .027).
- **One-way ANOVA** (F, df, p) and **linear regression** (slope, intercept, R², slope SE, slope p).
- **Uncertainty propagation** — enter a formula and `name = value ± uncertainty` lines; get the result with its **combined 1σ uncertainty** (first-order quadrature) and the dominant contributor.
- **Power & sample size** — the n per group that reaches a target power, or the power at a given n, for the two-sample and paired/one-sample t-tests (Cohen's *d*), one-way ANOVA (Cohen's *f*) and two proportions, from the **non-central t and F distributions** (verified against G*Power); the answer comes with its assumptions and a power curve with the chosen n marked.
- **Exact rank tests** — Mann–Whitney U and Wilcoxon signed-rank report the **exact p** (the permutation distribution, conditional on any ties) up to n = 60, with the normal approximation printed beside it, U₁/U₂ or W⁺/W⁻, the two-sided rule, and the smallest p the sample size can reach ("With 3 v 3, no two-sided p below 0.100000 is possible" — the approximation used to print 0.081).
- **Repeated-measures ANOVA** — one within factor, optionally one between factor (mixed design): the ANOVA table, **Mauchly's test** (R's form; refused by name when there are fewer subjects than conditions), **Greenhouse–Geisser and Huynh–Feldt ε** with corrected df ("F(1.60, 11.19) = 3.794, p = 0.0626"), which p it leads with and why (Girden's ε < 0.75 rule, never Mauchly's p), partial η², Holm-adjusted paired comparisons, and a **profile plot** with within-subject (Cousineau–Morey) 95% CIs and each subject's line.
- **Publishable OLS (econometrics)** — paste or read a table with a header row of names, write the model as `y ~ x1 + x2`, and choose **classical, HC0–HC3 (HC1 = Stata robust), clustered** (Stata's finite-sample factor, t with G − 1 df) or **Newey–West HAC** standard errors: coefficients with SE, t, p and 95% CI as a Word table, R², adjusted R², the Wald F, root MSE, AIC/BIC, and a diagnostics table (Durbin–Watson, Breusch–Pagan (Koenker), White, VIF, Ramsey RESET, Jarque–Bera) with a residual-vs-fitted plot. **Regression table**: several models side by side in the journal layout (coefficients with stars, SEs in parentheses, N, R², SE type, legend line), inserted as a real Word table. Checked against statsmodels on Longley, Grunfeld and stack-loss.
- **Cox regression** — time, event and covariates (a header line names them; text columns from a table become indicators with the reference named): β, **hazard ratio with 95% CI**, the likelihood-ratio, Wald and score tests, Harrell's concordance and a **proportional-hazards check**, Efron or Breslow ties. Drawn as a hazard-ratio **forest plot on a log axis** (so HR 0.5 and HR 2 sit equally far from 1) plus adjusted survival curves per level. Too few events per parameter, separation, start/stop data and 1/2 status coding are refused by name rather than fitted.
- **Typed decimal commas are read and said.** `1,5 2,3` in a list is 1.5 and 2.3 (the same rule a table cell follows) and the result says so; `1, 5` and `1,5,9` are still lists. Survival rows must be exactly "time event", so a typed id or start/stop column is refused instead of silently becoming the time.
- **Every calculator reads the table at your cursor.** Click inside a Word table and press **Use table at cursor**: groups one per column (a header row names them) or as label + value rows, repeated measures one row per subject or in long format (Subject | Time | Score, pivoted and said), survival as time and event columns (plus a group column for Log-rank), regression rows with the response first, a contingency table of counts, and two-way ANOVA as factor A / factor B / value rows or a crosstab with replicate lists in the cells. The status line reports which column became what, and an ambiguous layout is refused with the layout that would work — never guessed.
- p-values use the regularized incomplete beta (Student-t and F distributions). Insert any result at the cursor. Analysis aid — verify before publishing.

## 📊 Table → Chart — patent figures & PowerPoint from a Word table
- Click anywhere **inside a table** in your document, press **Read selected table** — the add-in **auto-picks** the representation that fits its shape (chart, flowchart, block diagram, or table figure) and tells you why. Change it any time in **Show as**.
- **Charts** (numeric tables) — column, bar (horizontal), line, area, **scatter**, **stacked column / bar / area**, pie, doughnut. First column = category labels, first row = series names (when it's text); numbers may include `$`/`€`/`£`, `%`, thousands commas, units (`12 kg`), and accountant-style `(1,200)` negatives. Cells that aren't numbers are reported, not silently charted.
- **Heat maps** — a numeric table rendered as a grid of shaded cells, for comparing magnitude across two categorical axes at once (month × region, a correlation matrix, an assay plate). Sequential (one hue, light to dark) or diverging about a midpoint (two hues either side of a neutral grey). Never a rainbow ramp: it implies an ordering the eye cannot recover and invents boundaries the data does not have. The value is printed in each cell where it fits, a colour bar with numeric ticks always accompanies the grid, and the black-and-white figure mode uses a greyscale lightness ramp — stating, where it applies, that greyscale cannot show which SIDE of a diverging midpoint a value falls on. Non-numeric cells are left blank and counted, never treated as zero.
- **Candlestick (OHLC) charts** — one candle per row from four numeric columns, showing where each period opened and closed and how far it ranged in between. The open/high/low/close columns are identified BY NAME; falling back to position is allowed only when the OHLC invariants then hold on every row, so a table whose columns are in another order is refused rather than drawn wrongly. Direction is carried first by hollow (up) versus filled (down) bodies — the older Japanese convention, which survives greyscale and colour blindness — with colour as reinforcement and the legend stating the convention in words, including the East Asian red-is-up option. A row whose high is not the largest of its four values is a data error, not a market event, and no candle is drawn for it. No volume on a second y-axis: two scales on one plot invent a correlation the data does not contain.
- **Periodic table & atomic structure** — the table itself, a Bohr-model diagram, an orbital filling diagram with Hund's rule, and a per-element summary. Everything shown is either HELD and verified (symbol, atomic number, standard atomic weight — the atomic number comes from the order of the existing table rather than a second list that could disagree) or COMPUTED from the aufbau rule (electron configuration, shell occupancy, block, group, period, and which elements are noble gases — derived from where each p subshell closes, not typed in). **No measured property is filled in from memory.** Melting and boiling points, density, crystal structure, Mohs hardness, spectral emission lines, oxidation states are absent and are REPORTED as absent with the reason, because a reference that silently omits a property is indistinguishable from one that has no data. Element NAMES, MEASURED electron configurations, oxidation states, electronegativity, ionisation energy, electron affinity and atomic radius are fetched from PubChem by a committed script and CROSS-CHECKED — the generator refuses to write its output unless all 118 symbols match the already-verified held table in order. The atomic weights are deliberately NOT taken from it: the two sources differ for lithium (which IUPAC gives as an interval) and for the elements with no stable isotope, where each picks a different reference isotope. Where the aufbau prediction and the measured configuration disagree — the ~20 classic exceptions — both are shown and the disagreement is called out. The f-block sits outside the numbered groups because the group-3 question (lanthanum or lutetium) is genuinely unsettled.
- **Flowchart** (text tables) — each row is a step, drawn top-to-bottom with arrows: a first column like `S101` becomes the step's **reference numeral** (with a lead line, patent-style); a step ending in `?` is drawn as a **decision diamond**; Start/End rows get rounded terminators; a `Step | Description` header row is skipped automatically. Ideal for **method-claim figures**.
- **Block diagram** (hierarchy) — each row is a path, e.g. `System 10 | Controller 20 | CPU 22`; shared parents merge into one **connected box tree** with orthogonal connectors. Leave a cell blank to repeat the value above (merged cells work). Ideal for **apparatus figures**.
- **Table figure** — draw the **table itself** as a clean figure, for characteristics/reference tables where the table *is* the exhibit. Preserves **section grouping** (a group-header row becomes a band; a blank "section" column merges down and is dropped if redundant), bolds/rules the header, **right-aligns numeric columns**, word-wraps cells, and scales wide tables to fit. Handles the dense clinical-style tables (`Section | Characteristic | n (%)`) common in specs.
- **Reference numerals** — number figure elements for callouts, drawn with **lead lines** to each element (37 CFR 1.84(q) style — free-standing numbers, not a column): block-diagram boxes get **hierarchical** numbers (100, 110, 112…) with a lead line to the box, flowchart steps get 102, 104… on alternating sides, and the table figure gets margin numerals with lead lines to each row/section. Auto-placed as a **starting point** — the drafter repositions them as needed.
- **Insert as a figure** at the cursor — the graphic goes into your document as an image; optionally **also insert the data as an editable table** beneath it, so the text stays editable.
- **Insert as an editable Word table** — for the table figure, insert a native, fully **editable** Word table instead of a picture (bold/shaded header, shaded section rows, right-aligned numbers). Edit the text like any Word table.
- **Smart column detection for charts** — a leading **row-index** column (1, 2, 3…) or a mostly-blank **section** column is recognized and skipped, so the real text column is used for labels (and the section groups them); `8,408 (75.0%)` reads as the count and a lone `(75.0%)` as +75% (not −75).
- **Patent figure style** — pure **black-&-white line art** for patent drawings (37 CFR 1.84-friendly): hatched bars/slices, dash patterns + marker shapes for line charts, white boxes with black outlines for diagrams, all-black ink, plus an optional **“FIG. N” label** beneath the graphic.
- **PowerPoint export** — download a .pptx that stays **editable**: a color chart exports as a native chart, a **table figure** as a native editable **table**, and a **flowchart / block diagram** as native editable **shapes** (boxes, diamonds, connectors — labels editable). Big diagrams **paginate across slides** — flowcharts continue via off-page connector circles (A, B, …) with numerals continuing, block diagrams split by branch with the parent repeated — so nothing gets crushed onto one slide. Only patent-hatched charts ship as a picture. Optional **source table on a second slide**.

## 🧫 Bio/Assay — quantitative life-science tools
- **Curve fitting, offline.** Paste your data and the fit runs entirely on your machine (Levenberg–Marquardt nonlinear least squares) — no server, no GraphPad round-trip. Each fit reports the parameters with **standard errors** and **R²**, and draws the **fitted curve over your data points** as a plot you can insert.
- **Enzyme kinetics** — **Michaelis–Menten** (V_max, K_m) and **Hill** (V_max, K, cooperativity coefficient *n*) fits; **catalytic efficiency** (k_cat, k_cat/K_m). The classic Lineweaver–Burk / Eadie–Hofstee / Hanes–Woolf linearizations seed the fit so no starting guess is needed.
- **Dose–response** — a **4-parameter logistic** returns **IC50 / EC50**, Hill slope, plateaus, and **pEC50** (agonist and inhibition curves both fit the same model); **Cheng–Prusoff** converts an IC50 to the true K_i.
- **Receptor binding** — one-site **saturation binding** (B_max, K_d).
- **Everyday lab math** — **Henderson–Hasselbalch** buffer pH, **Beer–Lambert** concentration from absorbance, **dilution** (C₁V₁ = C₂V₂) and **serial-dilution** planning, and **A260 / A280** nucleic-acid and protein quantitation.
- **Reaction stoichiometry & yield (v2.137.0)** — the reaction is balanced first, then each amount (g, mol, a liquid volume with its density, a solution volume with its molarity, or a gas volume) gives the **limiting reagent**, theoretical masses, the excess left and percent yield. Gas volumes use the STP picked from a dropdown that names both (IUPAC 273.15 K, 100 kPa → 22.711 L/mol: 10.0 L O₂ gives 46.9 g Fe₂O₃; 1 atm → 22.414 L/mol: 47.5 g); a liquid volume without a density is refused ("a liquid's density is never assumed to be 1 g/mL").
- Insert the result as text and, for the fits, the fitted-curve figure. Analysis aid — verify before publishing.
