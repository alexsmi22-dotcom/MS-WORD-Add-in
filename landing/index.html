What it inserts

Finished output, not a placeholder.

Every result lands as native Word content you can edit, restyle and cross-reference. Nothing is a flattened screenshot, and nothing is a stub.

The coupling topology is exact from the bond graph. Estimates are labelled as estimates, never passed off as measured spectra.

Recent highlights

What is new since the last release.

Every tile opens a full breakdown with worked examples.

Solve New

A no-code solver: solve equations (exact linear and quadratic including complex roots, numeric real roots for the rest), take a symbolic derivative, evaluate a definite integral, or work a word problem. Exact answers are labelled exact, numeric ones state their limits, and it says so rather than guessing when it cannot parse.

NMR: J-coupling & 2D New

The ¹H predictor now resolves coupling constants and multiplet names (dd, td and so on), and two new 2D maps join the Spectra tool: COSY (¹H–¹H) and HSQC (¹H–¹³C). The coupling topology is exact from the bond graph. The J values are typical literature figures, and the caveats say so.

Sequence Map New

Open a GenBank, FASTA or SnapGene file and insert an annotated linear or circular map, with features, topology and restriction sites, in a black-and-white patent-figure style. Files are read on your machine. Nothing is uploaded.

Align New

Pairwise alignment with the algorithms EMBOSS runs: Needleman–Wunsch (global) and Smith–Waterman (local), affine gaps, BLOSUM62 or DNA scoring. Reports identity, similarity and gaps, and warns in the twilight zone where unrelated proteins still align.

Analyze, the MATLAB-style workbench

The no-code numerical core: matrix math (solve A·x = b, inverse, eigenvalues including complex, QR, SVD, expressions), optimization, FFT, a stiff-aware ODE solver, and raw data turned into correlations, trends and insights. Results come back as editable tables and charts.

Compound library Updated

359 named structures with every SMILES verified against PubChem: violacein, morphine, cholesterol, paclitaxel, chlorophyll, heme and more.

Private by design

Nothing you type leaves your computer.

Every tool runs locally. Structures, spectra, sequences, statistics and citations are computed on your machine, so unfiled specifications and client matters never touch a server.

There is one exception, and it is optional. The online name to structure lookup stays off until you use it, and it asks for consent before sending a compound name. Everything else works with the network disconnected.

Works on Word for Windows and macOS (Microsoft 365 or 2019 and later).

Get it

Three steps, about two minutes.

STEP 1

Download the install pack for your operating system from the install folder.

STEP 2

Unzip it and run the installer. Full steps are in the install guide.

STEP 3

Open Word, then Insert → Add-ins → JurisLab.
